A book by Ken Stanely and Joel Lehman about novelty search and the downsides of objective thinking. Here's [a talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VDuF4onPmuE) about the subject as well.

Objective thinking is reductionist, in that we set a difficult goal and then we assume that we can break that goal into smaller subgoals until we have a next step to make.

Objective thinking is not always complexity-pilled, because it relies on making a prediction about what we can do. Predicting anything in a chaotic environment is very difficult. And people  know this: ask any software engineer, researcher, or anyone at all how well they can predict how much time it will take them to complete a task and they will tell you that it's so hard it's often not worth trying.

Objective thinking is basically saying "the joy is in the destination, not the journey", whereas [novelty-search](./novelty-search.md) is saying "the joy is in the journey, not the destination".

> You have the right to follow your passions (p.18)

> Not everything in life requires an objective justification (p.18)

Novelty search encourages diversity of people and ideas. It encourages you to be adaptable and to keep an open mind. It encourages you to be curious about the world around you and to always try and see where your current stepping stone might lead to, even if it's very unexpected. It encourages one to seek beauty. It encourages one to confident and brave enough to make their own path. A very positive message!

> Stepping stones almost never resemble the final destination (p.28)

The road to the final destination tends to be unpredictable. This is why ambitious objectives fail: we arrogantly assume that we know how to measure progress in the world. ==Do not create false compasses==.

The Chinese Finger Trap as an example of a false compass.

We should have the opposite approach: only use objectives when we're _damn sure_ that we know how to measure progress towards our goal. In other words, we should use objectives only when we know we have a good map of the search space between us and our goal. The humble default should be novelty search.

Applied to societies: you can't get to a utopia by setting a goal. Measuring progress as "how close to the utopia we are" is dangerous and misleading. Instead, we should seek to have many diverse societies - many stepping stones leading to potentially interesting places.
