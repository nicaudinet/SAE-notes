A blog post from Nick Cammarata, a mechinterp researcher at Goodfire AI, that's been doing the rounds lately (Oct 2026)

---

He argues that the story that neurons are not interpretable due to polysemanticity and [superposition](./superposition-hypothesis.md) is not true.

> The leading explanation for polysemanticity was superposition. This proposed a new learning principle: when a neural network runs out of neurons, it stops grouping related things together and instead begins folding unrelated things together, simply because they don’t interfere.

Recursive generalisation

> Put together, the argument has four steps.
> 1. Networks use one learning principle, not two (if not everywhere, then nearly enough that it doesn’t matter for study), so there’s no blocker to beginning ordinary science on them.
> 2. With modern models and tools, that science goes much better than most people expect.
> 3. Understanding compounds, so it gets easier as the map fills in. At minimum, then, a network’s own mechanisms should be part of the toolkit.
> 4. Highly speculative: this might be most of what we need, and interpretability might be far more tractable, possibly in the near term, than we’ve assumed.
